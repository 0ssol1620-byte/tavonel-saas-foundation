## 6. Pricing, Paddle, and GPU-cost governance

The agreed product direction is a **hybrid**: subscription access manages workspaces/governance; prepaid credits fund GPU-bound processing. There are no recurring included GPU credits. This intentionally keeps exposure bounded when a high-cost task is submitted and creates a clear entitlement boundary between access and compute.

| Non-live proposed pack | Credits | Gross price | Purpose |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Starter | 100 | $12 | Low-risk entry and qualification cohort |
| Builder | 300 | $30 | Default team purchase |
| Scale | 800 | $75 | Higher-volume prepaid capacity |

Using the previously researched Paddle fee assumption of 5% plus $0.50, the smallest pack yields about `$0.0884` net per credit. With a maximum GPU allocation target of 25%, the gross cost ceiling is about `$0.0221` per credit. The draft rate mapping is one credit per 45 seconds on a 4090, 18 seconds on an A100, or 12 seconds on an H100. These are initial policy assumptions, not published promises. They must be recalibrated only from approved synthetic P50/P95 job-cost evidence after upstream sanitation and R2 qualification.

The GPU policy is intentionally restrictive: Flex starts at zero active workers and a maximum of one; GPU class cannot automatically upgrade; a job reserves 2–10 credits; workspace daily consumption is capped at 20 credits; execution timeout is at most 90 seconds; reservation TTL is at most 300 seconds; idempotency is mandatory; ambiguous/over-reserved execution goes to manual review; and a margin-floor breach pauses future dispatch. RunPod’s documentation states that serverless capacity bills worker active time and supports per-request execution policy overrides, so all final policy values must be server-owned and never taken from a browser request.[1] No RunPod endpoint, account credential, worker, or job exists.

Paddle remains wholly unconfigured. The browser did not have an authenticated Paddle vendor session, and no vendor account, products, prices, checkout, notification destination, signature secret, sandbox event, or live event exists. Until a user-approved sandbox configuration is completed, every checkout attempt must stay at `BILLING_NOT_CONFIGURED`. The webhook design uses HMAC-SHA256 on the unmodified raw body in the form `ts:raw_body`, constant-time signature comparison, bounded clock tolerance, event ID deduplication, and `occurred_at` ordering. This follows Paddle’s webhook verification and delivery guidance.[2] [3]

> **Billing invariant:** A browser return from a checkout success URL is never proof of payment and can never issue an entitlement or credit. Only a verified server-side Paddle notification may project billing state.
